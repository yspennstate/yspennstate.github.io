Math Brain

Agents that do research mathematics lose their work when a session ends: a counterexample found in one session is unknown to the next agent that takes up the same problem. Math Brain keeps that work. It is a store of mathematical cards joined by typed links, about 13,000 cards in October 2026. The cards hold definitions, theorems with their exact hypotheses, sources, and the agents' own proofs, counterexamples, computations and corrected statements. Agents read it through a recall tool and through a hook that serves a few cards on each prompt. New cards are admitted only when a held-out recall test shows they cost no answer. Every revision is kept, so the record can be audited.

The tests were paired: same model, same budget, same questions. On 1,000 questions whose answers are in the store, agents with the memory answered all 1,000; agents without it answered 538. On 100 questions about results in the store, agents scored 0 with no tools, 75 with text search over the files and 83 with the recall tool. The paper reports the nulls and losses too. There was no significant gain over agents that had the documents and a recall tool, and none on thirteen new open problems.

Current projects

I’m extending Math Brain from memory to agency, so that agents can choose subproblems, keep a record of their failures and revise their plans over long campaigns. I’m also recording lectures on my papers.
